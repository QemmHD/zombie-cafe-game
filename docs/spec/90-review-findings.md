# Adversarial review findings (canon-pass worklist)

Three critics (fidelity purist, senior engineer, completeness) returned **needs-work**: 86 problems (28 blockers), 28 missing items.
Root cause: 7 parallel specs disagree on shared constants. `00-canon.md` resolves every conflict; it is the single authority.

## Complete Service Loop
- **[major]** The counter-batch mechanic is an invention presented as fidelity. In the original, the finished dish SITS ON THE STOVE and is served from there after you dispatch a server to a counter/table/sink; the stove stays occupied until the batch is exhausted or burns. The spec invents: batch physically moves to a counter, burn clock STOPS on counters ('fidelity: counters are the safe zone' — uncited, no research support), one batch per counter creating a new parallel-batch economy, and the stove freeing early. This changes the core throughput math (counters, not stoves, become the production cap) and creates the invented 'rescue run' mechanic. The overnight play also changes: the original's overnight rule was 'return within the burn window,' not 'park it on a counter before you sleep.'
  - fix: Either (a) restore the original topology — dish served from the stove, burn clock runs until the batch is fully served, counters/tables/sinks are dispatch stations only — or (b) keep counter-batching but strike the fidelity claims, log it in the deviation table with owner sign-off, and re-derive the throughput/overnight math it silently rewrites.
- **[major]** Offline serving at 25% throughput with zero tips is a nerf BELOW the 2011 original: research says the closed-app sim ran at 1× (the foreground was the accelerated side, ~10–15×) and 'cooking a dish continues while the app is closed... a zombie serves it.' Pillar 2 says offline earnings matter. Combined with the deleted idle drip, a returning player earns a quarter of what the original would have paid for the same absence.
  - fix: Settle offline serving at 1× of the live throughput model (min(seats, servers) plates/min), tips excluded is fine as flavor; the burn window remains the true offline cap exactly as in the original. If 25% survives playtesting concerns, log it as a deviation with rationale, not silently.
- **[major]** Two self-contradictions: (1) the single-faucet AC ('grep-level check that no code path other than ServeSystem.deliver() calls Economy.addCoins for gameplay income') is falsified by the plan's own raids (kill cash, eats, chests), retaliation payouts, and M10 passive-income Special machines (Vending $25/hr etc. — a researched must-have); (2) the fidelity table promises live throughput at the 'same order of magnitude' as the original's measured ~760 plates/hour, then §8.5 computes ~7,800/hr endgame — 10× the anchor, waved through as 'acceptable', inflating the whole coin economy relative to every researched price.
  - fix: Reword the faucet AC to an enumerated allowlist (deliver, offline-settle, raid loot, Special machines, quest rewards). Re-tune endgame throughput toward 1–2k plates/hr (fewer parallel batches or spawn-table ceiling) or consciously re-scale late-game prices and log the deviation.
- **[minor]** Tap-to-dispatch omits the TABLE as a target: research is explicit — 'you tap a zombie or the chef, then tap a serving counter, SINK, OR TABLE.' The spec's §9.1 maps counter/sink/READY-stove/floor only. A returning fan will tap a table on day one and nothing will happen.
  - fix: Add tap-table → SERVING mode homed to that table (equivalent behavior to counter-dispatch, biased to that table's seats).

## iso-engine
- **[blocker]** Turn-penalized A* is stated over tile-keyed nodes, but with a turn penalty the optimal g to a tile depends on arrival direction; a plain closed-set A* can permanently close a tile via a same-cost-worse-direction path and return a route with extra turns. AC-9 ('(0,0)→(5,5) has exactly one direction change') and 'optimal w.r.t. the penalized metric' are not guaranteed by the described algorithm. Additionally the start node's 'previous direction' is undefined (findPath takes no facing), so first-step penalty semantics — and thus byte-identical determinism across walkers with different facings — are underspecified.
  - fix: Search over (tile, arrivalDir) states — 4× node count, still trivial at 272 tiles — with per-state closed set. Define start state as a virtual 'no direction' (first step free). Add a property test: for random obstacle grids, returned turn count equals brute-force-minimum turn count among minimum-length paths.
- **[major]** canPlace's 'character' check races mid-step walkers: a walker <50% through a step has currentTile()=round(pos)=origin tile, so placing furniture on its committed destination tile validates green; the walker then 'finishes its in-flight step to the next tile center' — inside the new furniture. The spec's own guarantee ('canPlace guarantees no cell under a walker gets occupied') is violated by its own stepping rule.
  - fix: canPlace must test each covered cell against BOTH walker.currentTile() and the walker's committed in-flight destination tile (expose Walker.committedTile()). Add a unit test placing on a walker's destination at 30% step progress.
- **[major]** Seated customers are stranded by the pathfinding contract: a customer sits ON the chair cell (occupied ⇒ non-walkable) via allowNonWalkableGoal, but there is no allowNonWalkableStart — findPath's behavior when 'from' is non-walkable is unspecified (the 'invalid' status covers only out-of-bounds). LEAVING/LEAVING_ANGRY/flee-from-seat all path FROM the chair cell and are undefined.
  - fix: Specify that the start cell is always treated as walkable (standard A* practice), add it to the PathQuery contract, and add an AC: a walker on an occupied cell can path out to the door.
- **[major]** Depth tie-flicker at the crossover: character depth 2000+(fx+fy)·16+8 exactly equals furniture depth 2000+k·16 when fx+fy = k−0.5 — i.e., every time a character walks past a piece of furniture there is a frame of exact depth equality with Phaser falling back to nondeterministic display-list order (visible one-frame pop). The +8 bias also moves the visual crossover half a tile earlier than the anti-diagonal, so a character passing a 2×2 stove flips in front too soon.
  - fix: Make ties impossible: character bias +8 but quantize/offset character keys (e.g. depth = 2000 + floor((fx+fy)·16) + 8, or bias 7.99), and add an AC sweeping a character continuously past a 2×2 footprint asserting a single, correctly-located depth flip with no equal-depth frame.
- **[minor]** roomBounds maxY is wrong by 32px: the code returns 32·(w+h) with the comment 'bottom corner of (w−1,h−1)', but that corner is at tileToWorld(w−1,h−1).y + 32 = 32(w+h) − 32. The telltale '− HALF_H + HALF_H' no-op shows a drafting slip. Camera clamps, AC-1's random-point domain, and the 1280px height worked example all inherit the extra 32px.
  - fix: maxY = HALF_H·(w+h) − HALF_H; recompute the 17×16 example (2112×1248 with WALL_H=192) and any AC constants derived from it.
- **[minor]** AC-20 is unfalsifiable as written: it asserts a walking customer's 'sampled positions on tile centers', but §6.3 corner rounding (0.35-tile blend radius) and continuous constant-velocity interpolation guarantee mid-walk render positions are almost never on tile centers. A naive Playwright position sampler fails on a correct implementation.
  - fix: Assert on Walker 'step'/'arrived' events instead: at each event, pos equals a tile center within 1e-6; render positions are only asserted to stay within 0.5 tiles of the logical path.
- **[minor]** §5.1 vs §9.2 wall rendering self-contradiction: §5.1 assigns per-section depths ('wall sections: 1000 + index; wallDecor: hostSectionDepth + 0.5') but §9.2 bakes all wall skins into ONE RenderTexture at BAND.WALL — a single object has one depth, so 'hostSectionDepth' doesn't exist and wallDecor's +0.5 offset has nothing to offset from.
  - fix: Spec the baked model: wall RT at depth 1000, all wallDecor images at 1000+1+index·0.01 (or a flat 1001 band ordered by index); delete the per-section depth formula.
- **[minor]** Re-plan origin ambiguity in a determinism-mandated spec: on grid change a walker re-plans 'from its current (or next-center) tile' — 'or' is doing load-bearing work. Re-planning from round(pos) while 70% through a step can produce a path doubling back through the tile the walker has visually left, and two implementations of the same spec diverge on byte-identical-path claims.
  - fix: Mandate: mid-step walkers always complete the committed step, then re-plan from that tile; idle/pre-commit walkers re-plan from currentTile(). Encode in AC-14's test.
- **[minor]** Engine AC numbering is corrupted: two criteria are labeled AC-11 ('renumbered list continues'), and §12 risk #5 cites 'round-trip AC-11/12' for save migration, which is actually AC-15 — traceability from risks/milestones to ACs is already broken before implementation starts.
  - fix: Renumber §13 uniquely (AC-1…AC-21), fix all internal references, and give ACs stable slugs (e.g. AC-SAVE-ROUNDTRIP) so future edits don't cascade.

## service-loop
- **[major]** The offline-determinism AC is self-contradictory: 'fast-forwarding N seconds through settlement produces identical save state to ticking N seconds live with no customers spawned' — but settlement sells counter servings at 25% throughput while a live sim with zero customers delivers zero servings (payment requires a seated customer). The golden-file test as specified can never pass.
  - fix: Split the AC: (1) stove timers/burn deadlines — offline settle == live tick with no customers (this part is true); (2) counter sales — a separate golden test of the throughput formula against hand-computed expectations, plus an inequality test offlineEarnings(N) ≤ maxLiveEarnings(N).
- **[major]** The committed §10.4 tuning table violates its own 'profit/hr strictly decreases within a level' AC twice: row1 Finger Fries 480/hr < row2 Brain Burger 540/hr (acknowledged, but the fix creates a second contradiction — the 'first 20 dishes match this table exactly, Finger Fries price 3' AC vs the mandated post-pass making price 2/720hr); and rows 7→8: Vein Vermicelli 45s = 320/hr < Marrow Mash 50s = 360/hr — unacknowledged. As written, the economy invariant test fails on the shipped table.
  - fix: Run the monotonic post-pass over the whole level (not just row 1), regenerate the table from the post-passed output, and update both ACs to reference the same post-passed values (price 2 Finger Fries, adjusted Marrow Mash/Skull Stew). Never ship a 'match exactly' AC and a 'strictly monotonic' AC computed from different pipelines.
- **[minor]** The 'single coin faucet' grep AC ('no code path other than ServeSystem.deliver() calls Economy.addCoins') is falsified by design the moment raids land (kill cash, chests, eats) and by the retaliation win payout — and the API name it greps for (addCoins/spendCoins) is renamed addCash/spendCash elsewhere in the same spec and spendCoins in the infection spec.
  - fix: Settle the Economy API names once (cash/toxin ⇒ addCash/spendCash/spendToxin), fix all spec references, and re-word the AC as an ESLint-enforced allowlist of addCash call sites (ServeSystem.deliver, OfflineSettle, RaidLoot, Retaliation) rather than a single-caller grep.
- **[minor]** Risk register contradicts the FSM on the death-spiral guard: §15 claims 'no-seat penalty (−5) only applies when queue overflows', but §8.1 applies −5 to any QUEUING customer who waits 20s — which happens routinely whenever seats stay full, not only on overflow. The stated anti-spiral reasoning is false against the spec's own transition table.
  - fix: Either implement the risk register's rule (queue-timeout leavers exit sad with 0 delta; −5 reserved for spawn-refused/overflow cases) or delete the risk claim and re-verify the spiral in the 5-minute soak test with seats pinned full.

## Missing entirely
- The friends/social layer's SUBSTITUTE: the original had three recipe sources (leveling, raids, ordering/sharing from friends) plus friend-milestone Toxin (1/3/10 friends = +1 vial each). The plan deletes the social layer (justified — no backend) but proposes NO single-player replacement for the third recipe-acquisition path or its Toxin faucet; the research's 'order food from friends' loop simply vanishes without being logged as a deviation.
- Magic Fridge's mechanic: research names it explicitly ('Magic Fridge, 30 vials, gives one free dish/day'). The Presentation spec draws the sprite ('magic fridge, glowing') but no spec implements the free-dish-per-day rule — it would be a natural earned-Toxin sink.
- Toxin-upgradeable special stoves: research dim 0 says some cookbooks 'require special stoves bought with Cash and UPGRADED WITH TOXIN.' Progression's nine themed stoves are cash-only purchases with no Toxin upgrade tier — a lost sink and a lost fidelity beat.
- Outdoor decor: the researched Special tab contains 'pets, outdoor decor, expansion' — no spec mentions outdoor/exterior decoration at all.
- Industrial Barrel of Zombie Serum (50 vials, boosts zombie endurance) — a researched Special-tab item and premium energy-economy modifier absent from every sink table.
- Seasonal/event themed cafes: research ties specific rares to event cafes (Pina Collider from the Christmas Cafe) and notes 'themed content-update drops (Tiki update...)' as a retention hook; the plan has five permanent cafes and no event-cafe or seasonal-drop hook anywhere.
- The original 2011 dish names as shipped content: Mystery Meat, Handburger & Flies, Green Eggs & Sam, Dishwater Soup, Hobo Delight, Leftunders, Gello Mold, Sloppy 'Joe', Tumor Melt, Rot Dogs, Escargut, Fetidccine, Yucky-soba. The repo catalog (verified) contains none of them, and the Service Loop retune keeps the repo names — only the Build Order remembers the real menu.
- Rating-driven TIP quality as a stated loop in the Service Loop spec: research says higher rating brings 'better-tipping customer types'; only Progression carries a tip multiplier (1+R/200) — the Service Loop's tip formula (perServing × 0.05 × tipsStat) has no rating linkage, so the specs' rating loops close differently.
- The 'stars blink green/red summarizing while-away changes' beat in the Service Loop's welcome-back report (its report lists dishes/coins only; the away-rating blink — a researched signature — lives only in Progression's report).
- A single cross-spec constants registry (tile metrics, WALL_H, speeds, zoom clamps, rating scale, starting resources, spawn tables, save-version ledger). Nearly every blocker above is the same disease: two specs restating a constant. One generated 'canon.md'/tuning module that all specs cite by reference, with a CI diff check, is the structural fix.
- A unified offline-settlement engine spec. Three specs each define their own offline resolution (service: stoves+counters, staff: energy incidents, raids: revive timers/retaliation; progression adds rating decay) with no defined execution order or shared elapsed-window authority — order-dependence bugs (counter sales computed with servers that exhausted 2h in; rating decay before vs after bonus-star expiry) are guaranteed. One OfflineEngine that sequences all subsystem settlers against one clamped elapsed value, with a combined golden test.
- An input-mode arbitration spec. Tap-zombie-then-tap-target dispatch, tap-customer infect cards, tap-stove panels, edit/placement mode, camera pan-vs-tap thresholds, and raid commands all claim the same pointer. No spec defines the mode stack, what cancels what, or what a tap on a customer means while a zombie is selected (dispatch floor-move vs infect card).
- A test-time acceleration mechanism to replace DEMO_TIME_SCALE. Every spec deletes it, yet Playwright smokes must 'cook Finger Fries, fast-forward, deliver' and QA must exercise 12h cooks. Nothing specifies how CI/dev builds inject a scaled or steppable clock into the sim (injected Clock exists but no harness contract, no guard that it's unreachable in production builds).
- Failure/degradation paths for the asset and save layers: manifest.json fetch failure or atlas 404 mid-boot, corrupted-but-parseable layout data (placement out of bounds after a bad migration), localStorage quota exceeded on save write, and what the player sees in each case. Only 'wipe() fallback exists' is mentioned.
- Seat/station capacity interaction with expansion and door relocation: expand() can relocate the door 'to default', invalidating every seat's reachable-from-door status and all interactionCells-derived caches in the same frame as placements shift context — no spec covers re-validation ordering or events consumers must handle atomically (grid 'expanded' fires once, but SeatIndex, TaskQueue claims, and in-flight paths all react in unspecified order).
- A settings spec. Fragments exist (retaliation toggle in raids, audio channels in presentation, an M11 list) but no single doc owns: audio volumes/mute, reduced motion, screen-shake off, colorblind assists, particle/quality tier for low-end devices, the purist toggles (aggro ring off), language, reset-save, export/import. Needed by M8b at the latest.
- A telemetry/analytics decision. Nothing anywhere states whether the game ships zero analytics (a legitimate selling point worth advertising) or minimal self-hosted counters; meanwhile two specs' risk tables say balance will be 'tuned' post-launch with no stated source of tuning data. Write the one-paragraph decision; if zero-analytics, add an optional 'copy my anonymized stats' button to the feedback path.
- Seasonal/live events. The original ran themed holiday content; the plan's only trace is a cookbook named 'Haunted Holiday'. Either an explicit cut ('no timed content — everything permanent, decision logged') or a cheap local-clock plan (seasonal wall/floor skins + 1 quest line, no backend). Currently it's just silent.
- Art/IP licensing position and repo LICENSE file (detailed as a problem above, but it is genuinely absent: no LICENSE exists in the repo, README's license section is a placeholder, and no spec mentions generated-art usage rights).
- Browser support matrix and any non-chromium testing (WebKit/iOS Safari), plus a Safari 7-day storage-eviction mitigation — absent from all six specs.
- Storage-failure and corrupt-save UX (quota full, localStorage disabled, malformed blob) — the specs cover schema migration exhaustively but the runtime failure modes have no user-facing behavior defined.
- Return hooks without push: tab-title/favicon burn countdown, opt-in local notifications. The retention design assumes players remember burn deadlines with zero cues.
- In-game pause semantics: what the sim does during modals, during hidden tabs under 60s, and during a live raid when the tab hides — undefined everywhere.
- Empty-state UX: Storage tab with nothing stored, fridge with no stolen dishes, Zombiepedia at 0/105, world map before L4, roster with only the Chef, the store's stubbed Featured/Special tabs at M3 — none specced; the UI kit lists components but no zero-data states.
- A soft-lock/pity mechanic for the 0-cash dead end created by pay-then-cook + delivery-only income + no sell path.
- An i18n decision (even 'English-only, strings centralized for later' — currently strings are inline throughout the UI specs).
- A real method for the mobile-performance acceptance criteria: two specs assert 60fps on a Snapdragon-730-class phone as an AC, but no device-testing plan exists and CI cannot verify it; needs a CPU-throttled CI proxy plus a manual per-milestone device checklist.
- Deploy/update hygiene for the live game: atlas cache-busting, service-worker update flow, and cross-deploy save-version safety for already-open tabs.
